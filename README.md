# The Headlines We Wrote, and the Headlines We Will Write

An interactive reflection on the Whatcom County Justice Project community visioning exercise, the goals and headlines it produced, and the choices that connect those aspirations to what gets built.

## About this dashboard

In the early phase of STV's engagement on the Whatcom County Justice Project, a facilitated visioning exercise asked participants to imagine success. The room covered the walls with sticky notes, wrote the newspaper headlines they hoped to read, and articulated the goals they wanted the project to honor.

This dashboard reproduces those artifacts, synthesizes the themes they surfaced, and reads the headlines forward into two possible 2036 scenarios. It is offered as a benchmark against which the choices now in front of the County and the cities can be measured.

This is part of the Chuckanut Health Foundation's data and policy resource suite supporting an informed public conversation about the Justice Project.

## What the dashboard contains

- The nine original handwritten flip charts and sticky-note boards from the visioning exercise, viewable at full size
- A synthesis of the five themes the room returned to across all four exercises
- A "Two Pathways" framing of the distinct theories of success the room held simultaneously
- An interactive newspaper-format comparison of headlines the room wrote, projected 2036 headlines under a Pathway A scenario, and projected 2036 headlines under a convergent (Both Pathways) scenario
- Four key data figures and a forecast bed-range chart drawn from the Pasquo Planners and STV/EP&A consultant reports
- Five "Choices Ahead" connecting specific exercise outputs to decisions currently on the County's calendar
- A convergent vision read generously from the room's notes
- Source citations and methodology notes

## How to view

**On the web:** Once GitHub Pages is enabled for this repository, the dashboard is viewable at the Pages URL.

**Locally:** Download the folder and open `index.html` in any modern web browser. The page requires an internet connection to load the Chart.js library for the bar chart and the Google Fonts used in typography. All other content (text, images, layout) works offline.
